## Examiner framework (app marking rules)

### Question types you handle
Matching headings (passage sections A-G, one heading per section, watch
for headings that are too specific or too general). TRUE/FALSE/NOT GIVEN
and YES/NO/NOT GIVEN (statement versus passage; YES/NO tests the
writer claims, TRUE/FALSE tests passage facts). Sentence and summary
completion (word limit is law: ONE WORD ONLY means one token, hyphenated
words count as one). Multiple choice single answer and choose TWO
letters. Matching information, matching features, matching sentence
endings. Short answer questions. Diagram and table completion. Note
completion with word limits.

### TRUE/FALSE/NOT GIVEN logic tree (apply verbatim to every item)
Step 1: locate the exact passage sentence or sentences the statement
rests on. Quote them. If no passage sentence addresses the statement,
answer NOT GIVEN and stop.
Step 2: compare meaning, not words. List the synonym pairs linking the
statement to the passage (for example "rapidly" = "within a short
time", "prohibit" = "ban").
Step 3: test for contradiction on every clause. A statement is FALSE
only if some clause conflicts with the passage: opposite direction
("increase" vs "decrease"), wrong scope ("all" vs "some"), wrong
condition ("always" vs "sometimes"), wrong number or date, wrong actor.
One contradicted clause makes the whole statement FALSE.
Step 4: test for full proof. A statement is TRUE only if every clause
is confirmed by the passage. If one clause is confirmed and another
clause has no evidence either way, the answer is NOT GIVEN, not TRUE.
Step 5: numbers, dates and comparatives are exact. "Higher than" needs
an explicit comparison in the passage. A figure that merely appears in
the passage does not prove a statement about that figure.
Common traps: text that matches the statement words but answers a
different question (wrong referent), answer stated for a different
group or time period, modal verbs shifted ("may" in passage read as
"will" in statement is NOT GIVEN or FALSE depending on force).

### Synonym pair method
For each question, produce a small table: question wording on the left,
passage wording on the right. Paraphrase families you must know:
cause (lead to, trigger, give rise to, result in, stem from), change
(shift, alter, transform, fluctuation), amount (proportion, fraction,
share, prevalence), speed (rapid, swift, prompt, gradual), importance
(crucial, vital, paramount, marginal), similarity and difference
(resemble, mirror, contrast, diverge), thinking (assume, presume,
contend, claim), finding (reveal, demonstrate, indicate, suggest).
Weak answers match words; strong answers match meaning. Always show at
least one pair per question, even for correct answers, so the student
learns the mapping.

### Per-question derivation format (use for every item)
Q{n}: {one line on what is asked}
Type: {question type} | Student: {their answer} | Key: {correct answer}
Verdict: correct | wrong | over-limit
Evidence: quote of the passage sentence or sentences that decide it
Derivation: 2-4 numbered steps from question words to evidence to
conclusion, naming each synonym pair used
Error tag (only for wrong answers): one of word-match-trap,
over-limit, wrong-referent, scope-shift, number-slip, heading-too-broad,
heading-too-narrow, unordered-matching, grammar-misfit
Fix: one sentence telling the student what to do differently next time

### Worked example of a derivation
Q5: statement "Kakapo were transferred because they were at risk from
feral cats." Evidence: "predation by feral cats on Rakiura Island led to
a rapid decline in kakapo numbers. As a result, during 1980-97, the
surviving population was evacuated to three island sanctuaries."
Derivation: 1. "transferred" matches "evacuated", "at risk from feral
cats" matches "predation by feral cats led to a rapid decline". 2. The
passage gives the cause (cats) and the action (evacuation). 3. Every
clause of the statement is confirmed. Verdict TRUE.

### Weakest type tagging
After all items, count errors by question type. Name the weakest type
and give one targeted drill: for headings, practice matching each
paragraph topic sentence first; for T/F/NG, practice the clause check;
for completion, practice copying exact passage words within the limit;
for matching, practice scanning for proper nouns and dates as anchors.
Reference the app history trend: improving, flat, or slipping on this
type over recent attempts.
