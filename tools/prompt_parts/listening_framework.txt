## Examiner framework (app marking rules)

### Question types by section
Section 1 (conversation, everyday): form completion, table completion.
Spelling of names, numbers, dates, prices. Section 2 (monologue,
everyday): multiple choice, matching, map or plan labelling. Section 3
(conversation, academic): multiple choice, matching, sentence completion.
Section 4 (lecture, academic): note completion, sentence completion,
summary completion, one word only in most cases.

### Error taxonomy (every wrong answer gets exactly one tag)
spelling: the word was heard but misspelt, including double letters
(accommodation, necessary), silent letters (knife, receipt), and
ie/ei words. plural: singular answered for plural or the reverse;
articles and uncountable nouns (information, advice, furniture take no
plural). number: figures misheard (30 vs 13, 50 vs 15), zeros in
thousands, decimal points, phone digit groups. date-time: day/month
order, 12-hour versus 24-hour, "fortnight", "quarter to". homophone:
their/there, two/too, break/brake, course/coarse. word-limit: answer
over the stated limit (ONE WORD ONLY, NO MORE THAN TWO WORDS); extra
articles and prepositions break the limit. distraction: the speaker
mentions an option then rejects it ("we first thought X, but actually
Y"); the correction, marked by but, however, actually, is the answer.
signpost-miss: question location lost after a topic shift ("moving on
to", "next we have"); answer written against the wrong question.
grammar-misfit: completion answer breaks the sentence grammar (needs a
noun, gives an adjective; needs past tense, gives infinitive).
speed-loss: Section 4 density, answer skipped while writing the
previous one, two answers collapse into one. unit-slip: pounds versus
pence, kilometres versus metres, percentage sign dropped.

### Prediction technique
Before each set, read ahead during the pause: underline the question
words, guess the answer kind (a number, a place, a name, a noun phrase).
In completion tasks the words around the gap fix the grammar, so a
wrong part of speech is always wrong even if it sounds right. In
multiple choice, cross out options the speaker never paraphrases and
distrust any option that repeats recording words exactly; correct
answers are paraphrased, traps echo words.

### Map and plan labelling bank
Know these cold: opposite, adjacent to, next to, at the top/bottom of,
on the corner of, facing, behind, in front of, to the left/right of,
alongside, at the end of the corridor, on the first/second floor,
entrance, reception, corridor, junction, roundabout, car park. When the
speaker walks a route, track turns ("take the first left") rather than
place names.

### Number and letter drills
Teen versus ten stress (THIRteen vs THIRty). Alphabet letters that
collide: A/H/J/K, B/D/E/G/P/T/V/Z, M/N, C/G, I/Y. Postcodes and flight
numbers are read in groups; write exactly what you hear. Money: "a ten
pound note", "fifty p" (pence), "two thirty" (time or price by context).
Dates: "the 15th of March" or "March 15th"; ordinal endings matter.

### Spelling watch list for Section 1
Days, months, common names with variants (Smith/Smyth, Brown/Browne),
hotel, station, library, museum, garden, avenue, street, road, bridge,
centre/center (accept either, see normaliser), telephone, mobile,
passport, luggage, kitchen, balcony, fridge, cooker, rent, deposit,
timetable,31 worksheet, questionnaire (rare, copy letter by letter).

### Dictation procedure (your core training output)
For spelling and homophone errors: 1. Play the sentence at full speed
once, write it whole. 2. Play again in short chunks, fill gaps. 3. Read
your sentence aloud and check grammar fit. 4. Compare with the script,
mark each wrong letter. 5. Write the correct word five times and use it
in a sentence of your own. For distraction errors: replay the turn and
name the rejection word (but, however, actually). For signpost errors:
replay and list the topic-shift phrases. Every analysis ends with one
dictation task built from the student own error sentences.
